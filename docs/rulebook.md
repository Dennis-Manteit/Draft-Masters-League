# Draft Masters League Fantasy Draft Rulebook

These rules apply to every manager in the DML Draft Premiership and are intended to give all teams an even playing field. The applicable NRL Fantasy Draft rules and DML league settings govern gameplay. DML’s additional rules on the Round 20 trade deadline, team stacking, loan deals and trade-backs also apply.

## 1. Competition format

**1.1.** DML is a head-to-head Fantasy Draft league. League matchups begin in Round 1 of the NRL season.

**1.2.** Each NRL player may be owned by only one DML team at a time. Managers select players from a shared pool in the draft, then manage their teams through trades, free agents and restricted free agents.

**1.3.** There is no Draft salary cap. A displayed player price does not restrict a DML team from acquiring an available player.

## 2. Draft day and player selection

**2.1.** **DML Draft Day is 1 February every year.** The 2027 draft is scheduled to begin on **1 February 2027 at 6:00 pm AEDT**.

**2.2.** The draft uses a snake order. Teams select in the initial order in the first round, then in reverse order in the next round. The order continues to alternate each round. The initial team order will be made available before selections begin.

**2.3.** A manager has 12 hours to make each pick and may select a player manually while their timer is running.

**2.4.** Managers should rank players on their pre-draft list before selections begin. If a manager’s 12-hour timer expires, the NRL Fantasy app automatically selects the highest-ranked player on that team’s list who is still available.

**2.5.** If a manager has no pre-draft list when their timer expires, the app makes a random automatic selection for that team. The selected player joins the team. Managers should prepare a list to avoid leaving an expired pick to chance.

**2.6.** Managers may make manual selections or use their pre-draft list for automatic selections. The timer and automatic-selection outcomes in rules 2.3–2.5 apply.

## 3. Squad structure

**3.1.** Each DML team has 17 players: 13 starters and four players on the numbered bench. DML’s established **1:3:2:2:2:3/4** structure means one hooker, three middle forwards, two edge forwards, two halves, two centres and three wing/fullbacks in the starting lineup, plus four bench players.

**3.2.** The starting 13 must contain exactly the following positions:

| Abbreviation | Position | Starters |
|---|---|---:|
| HOK | Hooker | 1 |
| MID | Middle forward | 3 |
| EDG | Edge forward | 2 |
| HLF | Half | 2 |
| CTR | Centre | 2 |
| WFB | Wing/fullback | 3 |

**3.3.** The four bench slots are numbered 1 to 4. A bench player is shown as INT1, INT2, INT3 or INT4, or as EMG1, EMG2, EMG3 or EMG4 when designated as an emergency.

**3.4.** Managers must maintain the required number of players and fill starting positions with eligible players. The four bench players may be any mix of positions. A dual-position player may be placed in an eligible position under the app’s position rules. Every team must meet the league’s squad size and positional requirements.

## 4. Matchups, scores and bye rounds

**4.1.** In each scheduled round, a DML team faces an opponent. The teams’ final Fantasy scores determine the matchup result.

**4.2.** **Bye Rounds — ON** means DML matchups continue during designated NRL Fantasy bye rounds. Managers should plan for unavailable players and may move eligible bench players into their starting lineup before those players lock.

**4.3.** During a designated bye round, a team’s score is based on its starting 13, subject to the NRL Fantasy app’s eligible emergency substitution rules. A bench player does not score merely by remaining on the bench. An eligible bench or emergency player’s points may count if the app substitutes that player for a starter who does not play.

**4.4.** Player points are based on the official NRL Fantasy scoring system and players’ on-field statistics. Live scores may change while statistics are finalised. The platform’s final scores determine matchup results.

## 5. Rolling lockout and captains

**5.1.** **Rolling Lockout — ON** means a player locks when that player’s NRL match kicks off. The captain and vice-captain lock at the kickoff of the first game of each round. Managers may continue to make permitted lineup changes involving players who have not locked, subject to the app’s rules. A locked player cannot be moved for that round.

**5.2.** **Captains — ON** means managers select a captain each round. Under the NRL Fantasy captaincy rule, the captain earns double points. If the captain does not play, the selected vice-captain receives double points instead. Both selections must be made before the first game of the round.

## 6. Bench and emergencies

**6.1.** **Emergency — ON** means a manager may designate eligible bench players as emergencies to cover starters who do not play, including during designated bye rounds. Cover depends on the selected position and the app’s substitution rules.

**6.2.** An emergency must be eligible for the absent starter’s position. A dual-position player may qualify at either listed position but may cover only one absent starter. If no eligible emergency covers an absence, that starting position scores zero.

**6.3.** An emergency does not replace a player who takes the field and scores poorly or receives a negative score. The player who took the field retains that score.

**6.4.** An empty starting slot is not guaranteed automatic cover. Managers must set emergencies correctly; an uncovered slot scores zero. Emergency calculations begin at full lockout, when the last game of the round begins.

**6.5.** If more than one emergency is eligible to cover an absent starter, the NRL Fantasy app determines which emergency is used under its substitution and priority rules. An emergency player may cover only one absent starter.

## 7. Team-to-team trades

**7.1.** **Commissioner Approval — ON** means a proposed trade between two DML teams requires the Commissioner’s approval before it takes effect. Submission of a proposal, or agreement between managers, does not itself approve the trade. The Integrity Unit reviews all trade requests.

**7.2. Round 20 trade deadline.** Team-to-team trade proposals must be submitted before the kickoff of the first NRL game in Round 20. A proposal submitted after that kickoff is automatically rejected, whether or not it appears suspicious. A proposal submitted before the deadline that is not flagged by the Integrity Unit will be approved before the end of Round 20. If a timely proposal is flagged and investigated, it may still be approved after Round 20 if the investigation clears it.

**7.3. Trade integrity review.** The Integrity Unit may investigate a proposed trade that gives reasonable grounds to suspect team stacking. Team stacking includes an exchange that appears to give one team a star player in return for a fringe player without a legitimate explanation. The Integrity Unit may ask the managers to explain the trade. A flagged trade may be rejected if the explanation does not resolve the concern.

**7.4.** The approval requirement and Round 20 cutoff are intended to protect fair competition as finals approach. The same review standard applies to every DML team. The cutoff and review standard are DML rules, not universal NRL Fantasy Draft rules.

**7.5.** An uneven trade, such as a two-for-one trade, may leave a team with fewer or more than the required 17 players. A team with too few players must use the free-agent list to fill its squad and restore the required positions. A team with too many players must reduce its squad to the required size and structure. While a squad does not meet the league requirements, the app may prevent moves that do not correct it and may cancel pending restricted free-agent claims in accordance with its rules.

**7.6. No loans or trade-backs.** Loan deals and trade-backs are prohibited. Managers may not transfer a player temporarily or trade a player back to a team that previously traded that player away. This prohibition applies even if the managers describe the exchanges as separate trades. If the Integrity Unit reports a proposed loan deal or trade-back to the Commissioner, the proposal will be rejected and will not take effect.

## 8. Free agents and restricted free agents

**8.1.** The Round 20 deadline in section 7 applies to team-to-team trades. Free-agent pickups and restricted free-agent claims remain open after Round 20.

**8.2. Free agents (FA).** A free agent is an available player who may be added immediately, provided the move meets squad and app requirements.

**8.3. Restricted free agents (RFA).** A restricted free agent is an available player who goes through a **48-hour claim period** before being awarded. Other DML teams may submit a claim during that period.

**8.4. RFA Order — Reverse Ladder.** If more than one team claims the same restricted free agent, priority follows reverse competition ladder order: the team in last place has first priority and the team in first place has last priority. After a team’s claim is approved, that team moves to the end of the RFA priority order.

**8.5.** Managers must maintain an eligible squad when acquiring players.

## 9. Finals

**9.1. Finals — Top 8.** The eight highest-ranked teams on the DML ladder at the end of the regular season qualify for the championship finals. Throughout the regular season, if teams are tied on competition points, the team with more **Points For** takes the higher ladder position. This ranking also determines the final top eight. Teams outside the top eight do not qualify.

**9.2.** DML uses the Fantasy top-eight, NRL-style, four-week finals series. The finals take place during the last four rounds of the NRL regular season, with the DML Grand Final played in the final NRL regular-season round.

**9.3.** If a knockout final ends with equal Fantasy scores, the team that finished higher on the final regular-season ladder wins the tie and advances.
